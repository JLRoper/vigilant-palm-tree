# Settlements

<!-- docs:verified 2026-11-03 -->

The player's claim on the world. Settlements come in two forms: **initial castles** (pre-placed at game start via `castlePlacement`) and **charter-founded settlements** (created by heroes via expedition).

## Initial castles

At game start, castles are placed on the map (configurable via `castleSeed`/`castleCount`; `defaultCastleCountForMap` and `maxCastleCountForMap` in `packages/engine/src/map/castlePlacement.ts` scale the default and maximum castle counts by $\sqrt{N/432}$ on maps larger than `24×18` — up to 18 default / 45 max on `huge` 72×54 and 30 default / 75 max on `epic` 120×90 — and `generateCastles` verifies passable BFS connectivity via `buildReachableMask` so castles never spawn in isolated pockets). Castle candidates also skip river cells (every kind), so a castle never blocks or floods on a riverbank. Each faction gets one. These are Level 1–3 settlements with pre-computed resource rates, city spots, and mines.

## Charter settlements (✅ implemented)

A hero standing on a friendly settlement can initiate a **charter expedition** to found a new settlement at a distant hex.

### Cost

Paid at initiation time, deducted immediately:
- **2500 Gold** (from hero's purse)
- **20 Wood** (from provisioning settlement's warehouse)
- **15 Stone** (from provisioning settlement's warehouse)

If the hero is defeated during travel or construction, all costs are forfeited.

### Process

1. **Provision** (instant): costs deducted. Hero enters `"traveling"` phase.
2. **Travel** (1+ turns): hero auto-paths one hex-step per owner-turn toward target. Vulnerable to attack.
3. **Construction** (10 days): hero is stationary at target. `daysRemaining` decrements each `advanceRound`. Vulnerable to attack.
4. **Complete**: settlement appears as Level 1 with population 50, empty warehouse, 0 gold, morale 50, `autoTrade: false` (now the default for *every* settlement — initial castles included, since the 2026-10-02 rework; see [economy.md](./economy.md) → Trade routes & caravans), generated city spots (which may now include **food** spots, terrain-biased — see [resource-gathering.md](./resource-gathering.md) §8).

### Placement rules

- Target hex must be passable terrain
- Minimum 4 hexes from any existing settlement
- Not occupied by another hero or active charter target
- No movement-range limit — hero walks there over multiple turns

### Limits

- **No cap** on number of settlements per player
- Voluntary cancellation not allowed
- **AI chartering is live:** the server-side AI driver can start and advance charter expeditions using the same cost, distance, travel, and construction rules as human players (B3; see [multiplayer.md](./multiplayer.md)).

### Hero state during charter

- `isChartering: true` / `charterId` set — hero cannot be manually controlled
- Traveling: auto-paths each turn via `advanceAutoTravel()` in `TurnController`
- Constructing: stationary, `daysRemaining` decrements per round
- Defeat in any phase → charter lost, costs forfeited

## Levels

✅ **Locked.** Three levels ship in v1 UI. Level scales both resource yield and gold tax (population × tax = base gold income), and unlocks a larger city-view grid.

| Level | Tier label | Population cap (level base) | Gold tax/turn | City grid |
|-------|------------|-----------------------------|---------------|-----------|
| 1     | Settlement | 500                         | 1g/head       | 5×5       |
| 2     | Town       | 1,500                       | 2g/head       | 10×10     |
| 3     | Castle     | 5,000                       | 3g/head       | 15×15     |

These figures are the **level base** cap (`POP_BY_LEVEL`, `packages/engine/src/economy/settlementRates.ts`). The cap a settlement actually grows to is the level base **plus** the population every completed house / farmhouse / apartment standing in its **districts** grants (+300 / +1,000 / +1,500 for a House at L1/L2/L3, `populationByLevel` in the building registry) — `settlementPopulationCap` in `packages/engine/src/settlement/populationGrowth.ts`, the same function the settlement panel prints and the weekly growth clamps to. Housing inside the **main city grid** raises no cap: `districtPopulationBonus` walks `s.districts` only. So a level-1 town is capped at 500 with an empty district and at 800 with a single house in one.

Charter-founded settlements always start at Level 1 with population 50 (not 500). The level also gates **which buildings the settlement may place** — see *Building tier gates* below.

Resource rates scale with settlement level and nearby map resource tiles. Source: [`packages/engine/src/economy/settlementRates.ts`](../packages/engine/src/economy/settlementRates.ts) and the engine settlement reducers; client visual settlement entities remain under `src/entities/`.

### Building tier gates (2026-11-03)

A settlement's level gates which buildings it may place. The requirement is **declared with the building** (`requiredLevel` on its registry entry, default 1 — `packages/engine/src/buildingRegistry.ts`) and enforced in the one placement gate, beside the faction rule: `applyPlaceBuildings` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/placeBuildings.ts`) refuses a NEW placement with reason `settlement_level_too_low` when `buildingLevelAllowed(kind, s.level)` is false. `buildingRequiredLevel` / `buildingLevelAllowed` are the accessors. The server's command handler, the client's optimistic commit and the AI driver all reach it through that one reducer; the city view's palette states the same rule on the row (*"Requires a level 2 town — upgrade this settlement to place it"*, `src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildEntryStatus.ts`) and leaves the row in the list, because the tier is what buys it.

Only a **new** placement is gated. A building already standing in the settlement round-trips untouched — the city view's cart re-sends the whole set on every commit, so refusing what the settlement already holds would make it unbuildable, and a save written before this rule keeps working. A **district** is gated by the level of the settlement that owns it, so a level-3 castle's district may hold a level-3 building and a level-1 keep's may not.

The bands, over the 44 registry entries (16 / 18 / 10):

| Tier | `requiredLevel` | Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Settlement (5×5 grid) | 1 (default; no declaration) | townHall, house, tower, farmField, farmhouse, granary, market, smithy, mine, stoneMine, ironMine, goldMine, woodcutterHut, + each faction's starter troop building (crypt / delversHall / seedlingGrove) |
| 2 — Town (10×10) | 2 | apartment, warehouse, bank, treasury, arcaneFont, shipyard, + the middle troop buildings (barracks, archeryRange, stables, huntingLodge / ossuary, wraithBarrows / forgeHall, gunnersRedoubt, ramPens / groveSanctum, warrenLodge, sylvanStables) |
| 3 — Castle (15×15) | 3 | mageGuild, eyrie, spireOfAsh, blackChapel, dragonBoneyard, golemFoundry, deepAnvil, hearthTemple, worldrootGrove, druidCircle |

What the rule closes, measured on `main` `5df1580` (where the faction rule was the engine's only placement gate): a level-1 keep could place its whole **faction-legal** set — 25 kinds for a human or ironmark seat, 24 for ashen or verdant — including the entire grand row (mageGuild 400g, eyrie 500g, spireOfAsh 500g, blackChapel 400g, dragonBoneyard 500g, golemFoundry 500g, deepAnvil 500g, hearthTemple 400g, worldrootGrove 500g, druidCircle 400g — every one of them 6 build days) inside its 25-cell grid. At the far end, an L3 castle's control reach (level 3, plus its own town hall's +3) is **6 hexes**: 127 hexes, 126 of which can carry a district, i.e. **3,150 district city-grid cells** beside its **225-cell** main grid — before the rule every one of them could take a grand building from turn 0. The gate is what makes the tier the price of the building.

The tier-1 band is not a taste call: it is exactly what the engine itself places at tier 1 (the free starter layout `STARTER_BUILDING_KINDS`, the startup district pool `DISTRICT_BUILDING_POOL`) plus the producers that keep the settlement upgrades payable — the L1→L2 upgrade costs 20 iron and the L2→L3 upgrade 20 arcane, so gating `ironMine` or `arcaneFont` above those tiers would make the upgrade unpayable and the tier unreachable. The 2/3 split follows the registry's own rows: a faction's troop ladder reads starter (80–150g, 2 days) → middle (250–350g) → grand (400–500g, 6 days). Test: `test/engine/buildingLevelGate.test.ts` (the engine gate and the exact bands), `test/engine/buildingLevelAcceptance.test.ts` (the measurements above and the thresholds the gate leaves alone) and `test/screens/settlements/paletteLevelGateRow.test.ts` (the row a player sees).

## Population growth (✅ implemented)

Settlements grow naturally each week, provided they have enough food to sustain their current population.

- **Schedule:** Weekly during `applyWeeklyUpkeep` (day % 7 === 0)
- **Condition:** `warehouse.food >= foodRequired(s)` — growth only occurs when food is met
- **Formula:** `growth = max(1, ceil(population × growthRate))`
- **Cap:** `settlementPopulationCap(s)` — the level's base cap (500 / 1,500 / 5,000) **plus** the population granted by housing standing in the settlement's districts (+300 per L1 House, +1,000 at L2, +1,500 at L3; farmhouses +20 × level, apartments +150 × level). Housing in the main city grid contributes nothing. The settlement panel prints exactly this number as `Population <now> / <cap>`, with the breakdown in its tooltip (`populationCapTitle`, `src/screens/settlements/panelRates.ts`), so a capped town says so on screen (C-MINOR-2).
- **No growth penalty:** When food is short, population simply doesn't grow (morale decay handles the penalty separately)

The growth rate is configurable in Settings; the upgrade population gate is **not** player-configurable — it is the engine-owned constant `UPGRADE_POPULATION_GATE` (85%, `packages/engine/src/settlement/upgradeSettlement.ts`, issue #153):

| Setting | Default | Range | Step |
|---------|---------|-------|------|
| Population Growth Rate | 10% | 1%–50% | 1% |

Source: [`src/state/settings.ts`](../src/state/settings.ts) for the client setting and `packages/engine` turn/upkeep reducers for authoritative weekly growth.

## Settlement upgrades (✅ implemented)

Settlements can be upgraded to the next tier through an active construction process. Upgrades are player-initiated and require both population and Town Hall prerequisites.

### Settlement upgrade costs

| | L1→L2 (Town) | L2→L3 (Castle) |
|---|---|---|
| Gold (treasury) | 5,000g | 15,000g |
| Wood | 40 | 80 |
| Stone | 30 | 60 |
| Iron | 20 | 50 |
| Arcane | — | 20 |
| Construction | 15 days | 25 days |
| Req: population | ≥ 85% of the level base cap | ≥ 85% of the level base cap |
| Req: Town Hall level | ≥ 2 | ≥ 3 |

### Town Hall upgrade costs

| | L1→L2 | L2→L3 |
|---|---|---|
| Gold (treasury) | 1,500g | 5,000g |
| Wood | 15 | 40 |
| Stone | 10 | 25 |
| Construction | 7 days | 12 days |

### Process

1. **Pre-requisite check:** Population must meet the gate threshold (85% of the level **base** cap — the engine constant `UPGRADE_POPULATION_GATE` applied to `POP_BY_LEVEL`, not the district-boosted `settlementPopulationCap`; upgradeSettlement.ts), and Town Hall must be at or above the target level.
2. **Initiation:** Player clicks the upgrade button in the settlement info panel. Costs are deducted immediately from the settlement treasury and warehouse.
3. **Construction:** `daysRemaining` counts down each `advanceRound` — by **`1 + upgradeAccelEffort` days** (2026-10-17) when the settlement's `townHall` or `mageGuild` is **explicitly staffed** (max effort across the two kinds, so up to 2× with a full crew; unassigned/legacy buildings earn no acceleration). Settlement operates normally during construction (production, income, growth continue).
4. **Completion:** When `daysRemaining` reaches 0:
   - Level increments to target
   - Gold tax updates (2 for L2, 3 for L3)
   - Resource rates recalculated (pre-computed at initiation)
   - New city spots merged in (pre-computed at initiation)
   - Population and buildings preserved as-is
5. **Town Hall completion:** The Town Hall building level increments in the `buildings` array.

### Building upgrade footprints (C-MAJOR-3)

An individual building upgrade is a level bump, and some level bumps grow the
building's footprint: `woodcutterHut`, `granary`, `bank` and `goldMine` are 1×1
at L1 and 1.5×1.5 from L2 (`buildingFootprintFromRegistry`,
`packages/engine/src/buildingRegistry.ts`), and the placement path rounds that
up to a 2×2 block of cells. `startBuildingUpgrade`
(`packages/engine/src/settlement/upgradeBuilding.ts`) now validates the cells the
upgrade **newly** claims against every other building in the same container and
refuses the whole request with `footprint_blocked` before any cost is charged.
Before that check an L3 hut could swallow a neighbour's cell, after which
clicking the neighbour opened the hut's menu and the neighbour's own controls
were unreachable. Cells the building already occupies are its own, and a
pre-existing overlap elsewhere in the grid does not block an unrelated upgrade.
The check is start-time only: a neighbour placed *during* the construction
window is still not validated against the pending footprint (queued).

### Constraints

- **No concurrent upgrades:** Only one upgrade (town hall or settlement) at a time per settlement.
- **Upgrade persists through capture:** If a settlement is captured mid-upgrade, construction continues under new ownership.
- **Only player-owned settlements can upgrade:** The upgrade button only appears for the active player's settlements.

### UI

- **Settlement info panel:** Upgrade button below the warehouse grid. Shows pre-req status when requirements aren't met, clickable button when ready, progress bar during construction.
- **Building menu (Town Hall):** Upgrade button appears when clicking the Town Hall building (L1 or L2 only). Shows cost and disables when resources are insufficient, and its `title` carries the refusal: one `Not enough <resource> (need N, have M)` line per missing resource, in the build palette's own vocabulary (`buildEntryStatus`, `src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildEntryStatus.ts`). It also states the SCOPE of the click, before it is made (`src/screens/settlements/cityView/upgradeScope.ts`): *"Levels the WHOLE city to L2 — all N buildings, districts included — and the city's daily upkeep rises from 11 wood + 2 stone to 22 wood + 4 stone."* The engine's rule is `advanceSettlementUpgrades`' `Math.max(building.level, targetLevel)` applied to the main city AND every district, so the scope sentence is computed with `buildingUpkeep` over that same rule and can never describe an effect the reducer does not apply. Before it, the offer priced only its own one-off cost and its 7 days, so the button that doubled a young city's per-day wood bill read like a cheap upgrade (playthrough round 2b, E3).
- **A blocked upgrade names the treasury cap when the cap is the blocker (F-2/F-6, 2026-11-03).** A gold price above the settlement's `settlementTreasuryCap` cannot be reached by waiting: `applyEffectiveIncome` pays `Math.min(income, headroom)` and discards the rest at the cap. The town hall's L2→L3 price is **5,000g** against a level-1 cap of **1,500g** and a level-2 cap of **4,000g** (`BASE_TREASURY`, `packages/engine/src/settlement/capacity.ts`), and the settlement's own L2→L3 upgrade is **15,000g** against that same 4,000g, so both controls are cap-blocked at the level the cost is offered. `upgradeRefusal.ts` (`src/screens/settlements/`) composes the refusal lines for every upgrade control and `treasuryCapBlocksGold` / `treasuryCapBlockerMessage` (`treasuryCap.ts`) supply the cap sentence, which names the way out — a Bank or Treasury, `+2,000g` of cap per level, read from `buildingSettlementEffects` rather than hardcoded. The town hall's price list in the menu is the engine's own `TOWN_HALL_COSTS` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/upgradeTownHall.ts`), not a copy that merely matched.
- **One rate per resource, per panel (F-3, 2026-11-03).** The settlement panel's top section renders `daily.food` and its WAREHOUSE block iterates `daily.rows`; the two are the **same entry object** (`settlementDailyRates`, `src/screens/settlements/panelRates.ts`), so the two surfaces cannot print different rates for one settlement. An under-construction producer contributes `0` there, exactly as `producerTurnOutput` pays `0`, so during a construction window the printed rate is the population's bill alone — the honest reading the turn then charges.

Starting a town-hall upgrade (`UpgradeTownHall`), a building upgrade (`UpgradeBuilding`), or a settlement upgrade (`UpgradeSettlement`) is now a server-authoritative command (`server/app/commandHandler.ts`) — the client's local `@heroes/engine` reducer call applies immediately for responsiveness, then a matching command round-trip (`src/io/commands.ts`, fired from `src/state/turnController.ts` via `src/game/turnHooks.ts`) persists it server-side, the same pattern `StartCharter` uses (see Persistence below). `advanceSettlementUpgrades` (completion, on round wrap) has been server-authoritative since `EndTurn`'s pipeline was ported (`server/app/turnService.ts`).

Source: `packages/engine/src/settlement/` upgrade reducers, [`server/app/commandHandler.ts`](../server/app/commandHandler.ts), and the current panels under `src/screens/settlements/`.

## Capture

A hero walking onto an enemy settlement tile captures it — **only if the garrison is empty**. The settlement stays at its current level and continues producing. A **neutral** (ownerless) settlement behaves like an enemy-owned one for both the garrison gate and the capture reward.

- **Garrison gate:** if the settlement's garrison has troops — enemy-owned **or neutral** (the `unowned_settlement` gate was removed in the 2026-09-29 capture/garrison wave) — the tile walk enters the `SETTLEMENT_BATTLE` phase instead. A local-human attacker first confirms in the **assault modal** (2026-09-29 follow-ups, B5): "Assault on \<name\>" with You/Garrison army summaries and Assault / Auto-resolve / Cancel — Assault enters the manual arena (attacker vs *"<name> Garrison"*, title bar "Assault on \<name\>"), Auto-resolve resolves silently, and Cancel ends the client-local phase with the hero still standing on the settlement tile (garrison holds, capture deferred, no command submitted; re-selecting the hero re-opens the flow). A non-local attacker auto-resolves silently (result card/toast). Either way the result rides `SubmitSettlementBattleResult` (`startSettlementBattle` / `applySettlementBattleResult` in `@heroes/engine`). The direct `CaptureSettlement` command rejects a non-empty garrison (`garrison_not_defeated`).
- **Serialized capture (2026-09-29):** the optimistic local capture applies immediately, but its `CaptureSettlement` POST waits for the triggering move's persist (`TurnController.lastMovePersist`) — the server's hero-standing-on-the-settlement precondition only holds once the move has landed (this killed a live-verified ~50% `hero_not_at_settlement` 409 race, human and AI). A server rejection rolls the capture back (`rollbackCaptureSettlement` in `@heroes/engine`: owner/roster restore, clamped gold subtraction); `already_owned` — the server captured inline with a post-battle persist — is treated as benign, since the local capture already matches.
- **Settlement-battle loser outcomes (2026-09-29):** the attacking hero follows the hero-battle verdict rules — defeat removes them from the map (hero row + `heroIds` + charter folded), retreat keeps only its starting-edge escapees and relocates to the nearest owned settlement (stay-put when none is owned) stunned through the next day, surrender relocates keeping troops. Since 2026-10-21 retreat opens at battle round 3 for assaults, the AI/auto-resolved attacker retreats under the `outmatched` policy, and a retreat or surrender keeps the garrison's battle survivors (the garrison no longer heals). See [army.md](./army.md) → Combat resolution.
- **Post-battle capture:** when the victorious attacker stands on an enemy settlement whose garrison is now empty, capture fires automatically (`TurnController.captureAfterBattleIfNeeded`, wired on both the hero-battle and settlement-battle paths).
- Captured settlements produce for the new owner starting the next turn.
- Capturing is the only way settlements change hands in v1.
- **The client's `Castle` wrapper follows a capture (2026-11-02).** `GameStateManager.rebuildSettlementsFromState()` reconciles the wrapper collection against `SettlementState` on the per-frame state-identity diff as well as on `state:committed`, and the reconcile is identity-preserving — `Castle.syncFromState` (`src/entities/settlement.ts`) mutates the existing instance rather than reallocating, because `EntityMirror.syncWith` and the vision/territory caches hold those exact objects. A walk-in capture commits no `state:committed` (that would snap every hero), so before this the wrapper kept the pre-capture owner — and, by the same mechanism, every other mirrored field (buildings, auto-trade, level) — for the rest of the session (playthrough round 2, F4). The garrison has no such wrapper field at all: `SettlementState.stacks` is read live by the garrison panels.
- A player can recapture their own settlements by walking their hero back onto them (same garrison gate).
- **Active upgrades survive capture.** If a settlement is mid-upgrade, construction continues under the new owner.

✅ **Locked:** no other form of destruction. Settlements are permanent until captured — no spells, no demolition, no decay.

## Garrison (✅ implemented)

Every settlement can hold troops: `SettlementState.stacks?: Platoon[]` — the same `Platoon[]` shape as hero stacks (null-safe accessor: `settlementStacks` in `@heroes/engine`'s `units.ts`).

- **In:** `RecruitUnits` lands newly recruited units here (garrison-first — see [army.md](./army.md)); `TransferUnits` with `direction: "toGarrison"` pulls troops off a hero standing on the tile.
- **Out:** `TransferUnits` with `"toHero"` loads the hero's platoons — the hero **must stand on the settlement**.
- **Upkeep:** weekly (inside `applyWeeklyUpkeep`) via `applyGarrisonUpkeep` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/garrisonUpkeep.ts`) — the same `resolveTroopUpkeep` rule heroes run (per-unit catalog gold/food bill, gold from the treasury and food from the warehouse, shortfall taken as a morale bleed then weighted desertion after a 2-week grace). Full rule: [army.md](./army.md) → Upkeep.
- **Defense:** a non-empty garrison must be defeated before capture succeeds — in the manual arena for a local-human attacker (behind the assault-confirm modal), auto-resolved otherwise (see Capture above); since the 2026-09-29 capture/garrison wave the losing attacker suffers the hero-battle outcomes (defeat removes the hero, retreat/surrender relocate).
- **Neutral capitals start garrisoned (2026-11-03 — the garrison half).** A neutral settlement is a level-3 capital with nobody in it, and until this change it carried **no `stacks` at all**: walking a hero onto one captured it for free. Measured on a real `POST /games` at seed 12345 / 36×27 medium, the default board was nine settlements, **six neutral L3 capitals**, every one population 5,000 / morale 100 / `stacks` ABSENT — six free capitals in the fog were the whole middle game. `makeSettlements` (`packages/engine/src/init.ts`) now seeds each neutral settlement with `NEUTRAL_CAPITAL_GARRISON` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/neutralGarrison.ts`): one platoon of **12 pikemen (tier 3) + 4 archers (tier 4)**, 16 troops, power 168. Owned seats' castles still start with no garrison.
  - **Pure table, no new machinery.** The garrison is a constant — it draws no `rng`, so every seed's castle positions, names, horse variants, city spots and startup districts stay byte-identical, and `CURRENT_MAP_GEN_VERSION` does not move: 60 maps (2 sizes × v1..v5 × seeds 1..6, resource amounts included) hash identically to the parent. `SettlementState.stacks` + `settlement_platoons` (migration 016) already persist it, the walk-in refusal in `server/app/commandHandler.ts` already returns `garrison_not_defeated` for a garrisoned target, the assault flow (`startSettlementBattle` / `applySettlementBattleResult`) is how it is taken, and `applyGarrisonUpkeep` already skips `ownerId === null` — a neutral garrison never eats and never deserts, so it stays exactly the army the map was generated with.
  - **Strength, measured (`resolveBattle`, seed 12345, player's starting army = 24 troops / power 216 vs the candidate):**

    | candidate | power | starting army | added to win |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 8× swordsman (tier 2) | 80 | wins, −1 troop | already wins — trivial |
    | 12× pikeman (tier 3) | 144 | wins, −14 troops | already wins |
    | **12× pikeman + 4× archer (tier 3/4)** | **168** | **loses 24/24** | **+13 swordsmen = 2,600g (≈0.7 turn)** |
    | 16× pikeman (tier 3) | 192 | loses 24/24 | +10 swordsmen = 2,000g (≈0.6 turn) |
    | 20× pikeman (tier 3) | 240 | loses 24/24 | +13 swordsmen = 2,600g (≈0.7 turn) |

    A fresh medium game pays **3,500g/turn** (α L1 keep 500 + the seat's L2 castle 3,000), so the chosen 168 sits in the band *"the starting army cannot take it, one turn of recruiting can"*. Taking the capital returns a 5,000-population city at **15,000g/turn** — the gate is real but a fraction of the prize.
  - **The AI's route through the gate (2026-11-03 — the AI half).** The garrison alone made the capital unreachable for the AI: `attackerBeatsGarrison` (1.5× power) declines a 168-power garrison with the AI's 132-power starting army, and neither driver ever grew a hero's army — a real asymmetry against the player, who had two routes in. `aiDriver` now runs an **army section** once per round+seat, on exactly the player's route and at the player's prices: `pickHeroArmyRecruitment` buys units from the seat's own city (the same `eligibleRecruitSources` building/draft gate, best power per gold, the same 100g reserve) and `TransferUnits` hands them to the hero standing on that tile; `pickHeroArmyBuildings` places more recruit buildings through the same `PlaceBuildings` cart the city view commits (full placement cost, build days, first-fit free cells of the city grid) when the draft pools cannot cover the deficit; `pickHeroRefitStep` walks a hero home while its city is holding troops, and the sweep's collect-on-arrival takes them the turn it arrives. Sizing is one constant, `HERO_ARMY_TARGET_POWER = 346` — the **player's** realistic force at the turn it can first reach a capital, measured with `resolveBattle` against this very garrison (216 starting power loses 12/12; +13 swordsmen = 346 wins 12/12). The AI can never field more than a player at the same turn: it is the player's own number, every unit costs the same gold/warehouse/draft, and the target sits above the brain's own 1.5× gate (252), which is what makes a grown army attack rather than route around the capital. Measured by driving the seat on a real `POST /games` board (seed 12 / 36×27 medium the AI spawns 4 hexes from a capital): the hero army went 132 → 348 power and the seat took **five neutral L3 capitals by assault over rounds 5–11**, each capture resolving through the ordinary settlement-battle flow.
- **AI-held garrisons (2026-09-29 follow-ups, B1):** AI seats spend their own treasuries on garrison troops during their turns — threat-sized (`pickGarrisonRecruitment`: target power = 1.0 × nearby enemy-hero power within reach 8, floor 4, gold reserve 100), bought through the same `RecruitUnits` path and building gates as a player's recruits. An AI town left alone grows a garrison instead of falling to the first walk-in. **The two spenders and what the choice costs:** the garrison planner is threat-sized (target power = 1.0 × nearby enemy heroes, floor 4) and only spends on towns with **no hero standing on them**, so on the measured pre-fix board (seed 12 / 36×27 medium, seventeen driven rounds) a seat with no enemy hero in reach bought exactly **2 ghouls = 6 power = 80g** in total while its two castles sat pinned at their treasury caps (10,000g + 4,000g, Δ0 gold every round from round 6 on) — its whole income was being destroyed by the caps. The army section spends that same hoarded income instead, on the same buildings' draft pools; the garrison's own floor is untouched, and the two planners no longer compete for one pool because the garrison one skips the very settlement the hero stands on (the one the army section recruits from).

Buildings gate what a settlement can recruit; the newest are **huntingLodge** (placement 250g + 8 wood; recruits warhound for 180g; `defenseBonus: 1`) and **eyrie** (placement 500g + 12 wood + 8 stone; recruits giant_eagle 1400g + 2 arcane at L1, eagle_prince 2400g + 4 arcane at L2). The full building→unit table lives in [army.md](./army.md).

## Building roster — the newest and the storage roles

**`treasury` (new 2026-10-01).** 1×1, 400g + 6 wood + 8 stone, 5 days, upkeep 1 wood / 1 stone, `settlementEffects: { treasuryBonus: 2000 }`. It raises the treasury cap and does nothing else. No capacity code changed: `settlementTreasuryCap` already summed `treasuryBonus` over every building, so a new cap-building contributes on sight.

**`warehouse` is now 2×2** (four tiles) with `placementCost: { gold: 500, wood: 16, stone: 12 }` — the cost doubled because four tiles is ~19% of a level-1 town's usable space (5% at 1×1). `storageBonus` is unchanged at +600 × level on all five resources. The registry's legacy 1.5×1.5 L2/L3 visual override deliberately **excludes** it: `coversCell` uses `gx < b.gx + w`, so a 1.5 footprint covers only 2 cells — including `warehouse` would *release* two cells on upgrade and break cell exclusivity (the same reason `apartment`, `farmField`, and `townHall` stay out).

**`bank` reworked.** Its dead `goldPerTurn: 60` was removed — it was never applied, since only `goldMine`'s gold actually accrues — and its description now states what it does. It keeps `treasuryBonus: 2000` and gains **its own gold pot** (deposit / 7-day withdrawal delay / 5% flat weekly interest, capacity `5000 × level`). Persistence, the `BankGold` command, the `BankGoldMoved` event, and the client path are documented in [architecture.md](./architecture.md) → Bank pots.

**The three storage roles are not interchangeable:**

| Building | Raises | Also |
|---|---|---|
| `granary` | **food** storage +600 × level | A food **producer** too (+3/turn) — genuinely both |
| `warehouse` | **materials** storage +600 × level, all five (2×2 footprint) | +500 treasury |
| `treasury` | **gold** capacity only, +2000 × level | — |
| `bank` | **gold** capacity +2000 × level | Its own gold pot (interest + withdrawal delay) |

Caps are derived (`settlement/capacity.ts`) and **soft**: an addition is clamped to headroom, and stock above cap is never destroyed. `granary` and `farmField` moved from the palette's Civilian section to **Production** (both are producers); `farmhouse` stays in Troop Buildings because the palette's `recruits` check runs first. The palette also refuses a row it has no room for: `footprintSpaceBlock` (`src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildEntryStatus.ts`) disables an entry, and says why in the row's own tooltip, when no anchor for that footprint is free on the grid it is given — a level-1 settlement's 5×5 city holds 22 of its 25 cells with the free starter set and leaves no free 2×2 anchor, so every 2×2 row (Farm Field, Apartment, Warehouse, the built Town Hall) reads *"No free 2×2 space in this city — every place it could stand is covered. Raise the settlement's level for a bigger grid, or build it in a district."* instead of waiting for a click (playthrough round 2b, E4). The occupancy it checks is the placer's own `coversCell` over the grid INCLUDING the cart's staged placements, so the row and the ghost can never disagree. Every row that is not instant also states its build time and what the wait costs — `Build time: 2 days — its upkeep is paid while it is under construction, and it produces nothing until it is finished` (`buildTimeLine`, same file, from the registry's `buildingBuildDays`), with the same label on the row's hourglass glyph and its digit; before this the waiting time was an unlabelled number beside a glyph and the tooltip named only footprint and upkeep, so the two days of upkeep a site charges while producing nothing (`applySettlementConsumption` bills every building in `buildingUpkeepRequired`; `producerTurnOutput` returns null for one under construction) could not be priced before the click (playthrough round 2b, E5).

**A full store discards production, and now says so (playthrough round 2b, E2).** `addStockClamped` drops every unit of a resource that does not fit under `settlementResourceCap` — with no event, no log line and no HUD change — while the settlement panel went on printing the rate the settlement produces that resource at. Measured: a level-1 Gold hold at 498/500 wood read `+26.36/day` and its store never moved (three turns: 498 → 496 → 496 → 496; the tile rate adds `min(24, 500−498) = 2` and the town hall's own 4 wood of upkeep spends it). The treasury's identical cap was already announced; the stores now use the same vocabulary (`src/screens/settlements/warehouseCap.ts`, the sibling of `treasuryCap.ts`): the row goes amber even when its rate is positive and its tooltip carries `Wood is at its 500 store cap (500 held), so the 12/day it receives is being discarded until you spend some or raise the cap by building a Warehouse (+600 per level, 500g each)`; the End Turn toast fires on the transition into the cap (`newlyCappedWarehouseResources`), once per store per session (`applyWarehouseCapSessionDedupe`). Ownership follows the treasury rule — only the active player's own settlements, `seat === null` warns about nothing — and the cap-raising hint is read from the registry (`buildingSettlementEffects(...).storageBonus`: `warehouse` covers all five resources at +600 × level, `granary` food alone), so it cannot drift from what the buildings pay.

### Workforce staffing (2026-10-17)

Each settlement derives a worker pool of **`floor(population / 300)`** (`workforcePool` in `packages/engine/src/settlement/workforce.ts`; `WORKFORCE_PER_POPULATION = 300` since 2026-11-03, was 100), shared across main-city and district buildings. The divisor is the late-game lever — measured on `github/main` (0.40.0) with a level-3 castle whose 15×15 grid and both seeded districts are filled with housing, at the 80,000 population that housing grants, and a worker demand (Σ `buildingMaxWorkers`) of **275**:

| pool divisor | pool | pool / demand |
|---|---|---|
| 100 (before) | 800 | **291%** — the pool never binds |
| 291 | 274 | 100% — the last value that still covers demand |
| **300 (now)** | **266** | **97%** — three slots short: the castle must choose what stays dark |
| 400 | 200 | 73% |

A fresh city is tiny at this divisor (a level-2 town at population 1,500 has a pool of 5 against a demand of 23), which is why the auto crew below is load-bearing and was kept. Workers are assigned per building via the `AssignWorkers` command (`packages/contracts/src/commands/assignWorkers.ts` — reducer in `packages/engine/src/settlement/staffWorkers.ts`, rejection reasons `no_settlement` / `unowned_settlement` / `no_building` / `not_staffable` / `invalid_workers` / `invalid_source` / `pool_exhausted`), emitting a `WorkersAssigned` EngineEvent. An optional `source?: { gx, gy }` (2026-10-18) turns the command into an **atomic one-worker move**: the reducer validates and writes both buildings in a single operation (source loses one, destination gains it), so the press-and-hold drag below never commits a partial transfer; a transfer out of a legacy building still takes the display's count − 1 at the source. `BuildingDef.workers` **undefined = legacy auto-staffed** (effort 1) — an untouched legacy settlement behaves exactly as before. **An assignment writes only the building it addresses** (2026-10-26, C-MAJOR-2): every other building keeps the staffing it already had, so a legacy city stays legacy and converts to explicit crews one building at a time. The first touch used to convert the WHOLE settlement — normalizing every building to an explicit count and auto-employing the remaining pool across job kinds — and with a level-1 pool of 5 spread over 13 buildings that single click left the city advertising −12.8 wood/day where it had advertised −3.84 and turned +6.64 stone into −1.11 and +14.33 food into −0.53 (the measured turn deltas matched). New buildings placed into a settlement that already has an explicit count **or any district** start at `workers: 0`; new buildings in an untouched legacy settlement keep legacy auto-staffing. That district clause (2026-11-03) is what stops the free full crew from following the player into a built-out castle: the auto crew is a bootstrap, and the crew is counted and shown (`autoStaffedSlots` / `autoStaffedCount` — the settlement panel reads "auto crew · N bldgs (M slots), pool P", the workforce panel header names the crew and says it spends no pool). An explicit number scales the building's effort `min(workers / maxWorkers, 1)` and `0` shuts it down. Role tags per kind — not exclusive, the panel shows all of them (`farmhouse` = producer+recruiter, `mageGuild` = recruiter+upgrade-accel):

- **Producers** (goldMine, woodcutterHut, stoneMine, ironMine, arcaneFont, farmField, farmhouse, granary): output scales `round(base × cellMultiplier × effort)`.
- **`market`**: flat `goldPerTurn` × effort lands in the treasury (F2 resolved).
- **Recruit kinds (19):** each recruits from a per-unitTypeId **draft pool** (`draftMax` 3; `draftPerDay` 0.25 × `(1 + effort)` × tier multiplier reclaimed daily on the round wrap, `applyDraftRecharge` — tier-scaled since 2026-10-18, tiers 1–7 → ×1 / ×0.8 / ×0.65 / ×0.5 / ×0.4 / ×0.3 / ×0.2 per `TIER_DRAFT_RECHARGE_MULTIPLIER` in `packages/engine/src/units.ts`, so higher-tier units refill slower; catalog-less callers → ×1). Recruiting consumes the pool (rejection `draft_pool_empty` when the count exceeds `floor` of the pool) and still costs gold/resources; `draft` undefined = full pool (legacy). The client's **recruitment panel** (`src/screens/settlements/cityView/recruitmentPanel.ts`, model `src/screens/settlements/recruitmentPanelModel.ts`; ⚔ Recruit or `r`) collapses every recruit source in the settlement **and its districts** into one block per building kind, shows each unit's `+N/wk` refill rate recomputed from the live staffing of every building of that kind (7 × `buildingDraftPerDay` × `draftRechargeMultiplier` × `tierDraftRechargeMultiplier`), staffs recruiters from each building's own line (`−`/`+` committing `AssignWorkers` district-true), offers a per-kind **Buy all** that allocates most-stocked-building-first then round-robin (`allocateRecruitCounts`), and commits each allocation through this same `RecruitUnits` path with that source building's own coords + district — no new command or event. The panel also mounts the settlement panel's **Garrison** accordion content itself (`src/screens/settlements/garrisonView.ts`): one `GarrisonView` instance per surface, identical square tiles that drag-and-drop (within a grid swaps two slots, `ReorderGarrison`; between the garrison grid and the hero's army grid moves troops via `TransferUnits` into the dropped slot), both refreshed from the same UIManager pass so a drag on either shows on both. Its building list scrolls inside the panel and the panel is resizable (corner grip, size remembered).
- **`townHall` + `mageGuild`:** staffing accelerates the settlement's active upgrade timer — see the Upgrade process below.
- **Construction sites (staffed work, 2026-11-03):** a building under construction advances `buildRate(buildersOnSite(b))` days per round wrap (`advanceBuildingConstructions`, `packages/engine/src/settlement/advance.ts`), where the crew ceiling on a site is `CONSTRUCTION_MAX_BUILDERS` (**4, for EVERY kind**) and each extra builder adds `BUILD_RATE_PER_EXTRA_BUILDER` (a third), so a site builds at **0 / 1.00 / 1.33 / 1.67 / 2.00×** for 0–4 builders (`packages/engine/src/settlement/workforce.ts`, `staffingCeiling`/`buildersOnSite`). **A site with no builders makes no progress at all**: the stall is an absence of decrement (nothing is stored as negative progress), it survives any number of End Turns, and the settlement panel's Workforce row names it (` · 1 site stalled`; absent when none is stalled). The curve's top is deliberately the rate today's engine already reaches at a kind's own full crew (`1 + effortFraction` = 2.00×), so **an untouched building builds in exactly the time it always did**; the free auto crew (`workers` undefined) is a FULL crew, never 1 builder and never 0. A `buildDays: 0` kind (`townHall`) still completes on the first tick whatever its crew. **On completion the crew is trimmed to the kind's own cap in the same tick** (`workers = min(workers, buildingMaxWorkers(kind))`, before anything else reads the crew): the surplus returns to the settlement's idle pool, no worker is deleted, and no site is auto-staffed. A fresh placement in a normalized settlement carries `workers: 0` (stalled until staffed) and a bootstrap settlement keeps the auto-staffed full crew; **the CLIENT's row for that placement is the server's row (2026-11-03):** the city view writes the cart into its own state *before* the engine's commit runs over it (`UIManager.onClose`'s optimistic resource settle), and the engine reads any entry it finds there as an existing building whose staffing must be preserved — so the cart's fresh entries carry the same verdict into that write (`withFreshPlacementStaffing` → `freshPlacementWorkers`, read off the live settlement before the commit). Without it the new-placement branch never ran on the client, the row kept no count at all, `buildersOnSite` read that as the full 4-builder crew and the menu printed `2.00× (4 of 4 builders)` for a site the server holds frozen at `0` until the next End Turn replaced the state; district buildings follow the same rule, and an AI seat crews its own placements out of its own pool (`pickAiConstructionStaffing`, shared by `server/app/aiDriver.ts` and `src/state/turnController.ts` — an AI seat's settlement is never a bootstrap, so without it every AI site would freeze). **Its placement gate is priced in the same currency (2026-11-03)**: a site claims `CONSTRUCTION_MAX_BUILDERS` slots from the moment it is started (`committedWorkerSlots`) instead of the explicit `0` it stores — the crew the seat's own staffing pass will put on it, which is never less than the crew the completion trim pins — so the seat starts only what it can crew, and never fills its pool with finished buildings while sites it has already started still wait for a builder (measured before the fix: 31 buildings started against a 17-slot pool, 22 of 23 sites frozen half-built; after it, every AI placement is crewed in the round it is placed and finishes).
- Every other kind holds workers **inert** (parked). Per-kind caps (`buildingMaxWorkers`): producers 3 (farmField/farmhouse/granary 2), recruit kinds 4, market/townHall/mageGuild 3, house 1, apartment 2, all others 2. While a building is **under construction** its crew ceiling is `CONSTRUCTION_MAX_BUILDERS` (4) whatever its kind, via `staffingCeiling(b)`; the construction cap **raises no building's operational capacity** — a house that 4 builders raised still holds exactly 1 worker once it is finished.

**Daily wages (2026-10-18).** Population also accrues a wage bill: every active player's settlement pays `floor(population / 100)` gold per day (1g per 100 population, `WAGE_POPULATION_PER_GOLD = 100`; `settlementDailyWage` / `applySettlementWages` in `packages/engine/src/economy/consumption.ts`) at end of turn, after food consumption and before income. The deduction floors at 0 (no debt) and carries no morale penalty; it is not yet shown in the settlement panel UI.

Client: per-building worker stepper and draft-pool readouts in the city-view building menu (the green gold/food effect lines show the **current** staffing-scaled value — e.g. `+13.33 gold/turn (33% staffed)` — and refresh on every stepper commit), a floating workforce panel (`w` or the **♟ Workforce** button in the City Design box opens it — the box itself is a draggable floating panel with a remembered position since 2026-10-19; drag its chips onto buildings to add from the pool; geometry key `workforcePanel`, resized by the shared corner grip with the size remembered; each row shows `n/max` with every role tag under the name so long labels no longer truncate, hovering a row highlights that building's footprint so duplicate rows are distinguishable, and while the settlement is still legacy the header reads `auto-staffed (pool N) — first assignment switches this city to manual crews`; chips are disabled when no idle workers remain), a Workforce row in the settlement info menu (legacy shows `auto · pool N`; an oversubscribed pool shows `over pool` instead of a negative idle count), and city-view peasant sprites (cap 4 per building) that alternate a 1px bob between two states staggered per worker (2026-10-18; the renderer honors `prefers-reduced-motion` with a static frame, and the visual-regression suite pins reduced motion). Press-and-hold on a staffed building's iso-grid footprint picks a worker up (dashed source highlight; grid-aligned hover highlight on the destination) and releasing over another building **moves** one worker via the atomic `source` form above; drop on empty ground, the same building, a full building, or off-canvas — or `Esc` — cancels with no changes, and the trailing click after any drag does not open the building menu (panel chips still add from the pool). Drops resolve only on the game canvas: releasing over any floating UI (workforce panel, building menu, HUD) cancels instead of assigning the building hidden underneath, a click on a panel chip without motion is a no-op, and a worker pickup is not armed while the Build/Destroy placer is active. `Esc` closes the workforce panel before the city view, the panel closes with the view (no orphan floating over the world map), the last drag is cancelled on window blur, and city shortcuts (`w` / `b`) are ignored while a menu input has focus. Any successful assignment from any surface refreshes the open building menu (`notifyWorkforceCommit`), and the legacy staffing predicates the menu and panel read are centralized in the engine workforce module (`hasLegacyStaffing` / `hasExplicitStaffing` / `staffingBase`, plus `buildingLegacyStaffing` / `steppedWorkers` since 2026-11-03: a stepper steps ONE building, so it asks that building's own legaciness — the settlement-wide flag made `+` write 1 forever in any city holding a single auto-crewed building, which left the 2nd-4th builder unreachable from both steppers and the grid drag). Staffing is server-owned across the city-view cart round-trip: `syncCartBuilding` re-syncs `workers` / `draft` from live state (like `construction` / `bank`), so closing the view or committing a placement/destroy no longer writes the open-time counts back over assignments made while the view was open (2026-10-18 regression fix).

## Building persistence (✅ implemented)

Buildings placed in the city view are persisted to `SettlementState.buildings` (a `BuildingDef[]` array). Previously ephemeral (only existed while city view was open), buildings now survive close/reopen cycles.

- **First open:** a settlement with no buildings is handed the **free starter set** and it is committed for the player — `townHall L1 + farmField L1 + 2× house L1 + 2× woodcutterHut L1 + stoneMine L1 + farmhouse L1` (`buildStarterLayout`, `packages/engine/src/settlement/starterLayout.ts`), costing **10 wood + 2 stone** per turn (the farmhouse's 1 wood + 0 stone included; the second hut is the 2026-10-02 balance fix: one hut left the set net wood-negative, median −5 wood/turn; two land it at median −4). The farmhouse is the tier-1 peasant's recruiting building (25g, `minLevel 1`), so every settlement — player and AI alike — can raise garrison troops from the moment it exists (the AI garrison planner shops the same `eligibleRecruitSources` gate). This replaced a hand-off of `cityBuildingGen`'s denseUrban layout (~14 buildings, level-2 town hall, **no producer at all**, ~24 wood + 14 stone per turn) which bankrupted a player by roughly turn 12. Farm count is sized against the settlement's **own** food bill (its population plus the weekly bill of any starting hero standing on it — `starterFarmsNeeded(foodBill)`): the L1 keep asks 4 and places 3 (a 5×5 grid physically holds 3 beside the town hall; the accumulated surplus covers the rest), the L2 town (pop 1,500) gets 4 on 10×10, an AI seat's L3 castle 12, and a neutral L3 castle (pop 5,000) 11 on 15×15 (a 1-player game creates 4 settlements, not 2 — `castleCount` is floored at `CASTLE_COUNT_MIN = 4`, so two neutral L3s are seeded the same way).
- **Never twice:** `starterCityOnOpen({ existing })` returns an already-populated settlement's own array with `free: false`, so no `PlaceBuildings` is sent and the town hall is never dropped.
- **Close:** The full buildings array is written back to settlement state.
- **Generate button:** A small "Generate" button in the top-right of the city view replaces the entire buildings array with fresh generation. Useful for testing.
- **Town Hall at center:** The center cell is always reserved for a Town Hall building.

Source: [`src/screens/settlements/cityView/cityView.ts`](../src/screens/settlements/cityView/cityView.ts), [`src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildingPlacer.ts`](../src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildingPlacer.ts), and the layout/guard pair in `packages/engine/src/settlement/starterLayout.ts`. The city view's re-sync of its working cart against live state goes through the pure `syncCartBuildings` (`src/screens/settlements/cityView/syncedBuildings.ts`) — it refreshes `level`/`style`/`construction` **and** `bank`, so a pot that changed while the city view was open is not written back stale (unit-tested, because city view imports cannot be loaded under bare `node:test`).

## City districts (✅ implemented)

Owned settlements can expand onto non-settlement hexes within their territory (`controlRange(level)` in `packages/engine/src/control.ts`, resolved via `resolveControllingSettlement` and keyed by `districtKey(q, r)` as `"q,r"`).

- **Grid & spots:** Each district opens in `CityView` (`district?: { q, r }`) as a 5×5 satellite grid where all 25 cells (including center `(2, 2)`) are buildable (`Town Hall` is forbidden: `town_hall_forbidden_in_district`). `generateDistrictSpots` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/citySpots.ts`) generates 3 deterministic `"rich"` spots in `0..4 × 0..4`; when the map hex carries a resource deposit, `spots[0]` is guaranteed to match that resource.
- **Dense-forest wood bonus:** A hex whose terrain is `forest` and which touches a `denseForest` hex gets **one extra spot** — `DENSE_FOREST_BONUS_SPOT` (`wood`, `vein: "dense"`) — on top of its own spots, so its grid carries 4. The rule is the forest's, not the settlement's: no other terrain gains anything. Callers pass `{ touchesDenseForest: touchesTerrain(map, q, r, "denseForest") }` to `generateDistrictSpots` (client `GameEngine`/`UIManager`, server `PlaceBuildings`, and `districtSeed`'s startup districts), so the client-side optimistic grid and the server-authoritative one agree. Existing spots are drawn first, so a hex with no dense-forest neighbour keeps byte-identical spots.
- **No floating districts (2026-10-18):** A district must be part of ONE connected cluster rooted at the settlement's own hex — it must be hex-adjacent to the settlement centre or to another district of the same settlement. Chains count (centre → A → B). Enforced in `packages/engine/src/settlement/districtConnectivity.ts` (`districtConnectionReason`, `connectedDistrictKeys`, `disconnectedDistrictKeys`) and checked twice: the reducer (`applyPlaceBuildings`) refuses any commit whose RESULT leaves a non-empty district outside the cluster (`district_not_connected`, which is what also stops a player clearing the last building out of a bridging district and stranding the far side), and `server/app/commandHandler.ts`'s `PlaceBuildings` pre-checks the target hex for the precise reason before any cost is charged.
- **Water districts & the shipyard (2026-10-18):** A water hex is a district ONLY when it holds a `shipyard` — the one building that may stand on water, and the exception the connectivity rule is built around: water that separates two districts is bridged when a shipyard water district sits in between, because that water district is itself a link in the chain. Rejections: `impassable_district_terrain` (mountain always; water without a shipyard) and `shipyard_requires_water` (a shipyard on a land hex). The city view mirrors both (`districtTerrainBlock` in `src/screens/settlements/cityView/buildEntryStatus.ts`), so the palette disables the same builds the server refuses.
- **Startup districts (2026-10-18):** A settlement is BORN with its districts (`packages/engine/src/settlement/districtSeed.ts`, called from `init.ts`'s `seedStartupDistrictPass`): tier 1 gets none, tier 2 gets 1, tier 3 gets 2. Each seeded district holds 2–3 level-1 buildings from `DISTRICT_BUILDING_POOL` (never a town hall), placed on legal non-overlapping cells, with its own `generateDistrictSpots` set; a resource deposit on the hex picks that resource's producer (or a farm field for food). They are free and already built — the town the game started with, like the main city's starter layout — and every one of them satisfies the connectivity rule by construction (the first is always a neighbour of the settlement hex, later ones must touch the settlement or a chosen district). Seeding draws from its own per-settlement RNG stream (`mulberry32(hashString("startup-districts:<castleSeed>:<settlementId>"))`), so the map's own seeded generation — names, horse variants, city spots — is unchanged.
- **Economy & pooling:** District placements always pay full net cost (never `freeInitialLayout`). Completed district buildings pool into the parent settlement's resource production (`settlementProductionRates`, `produceSettlementResources`), gold income (`settlementIncome`, `effectiveSettlementIncome`), storage and treasury caps (`settlementResourceCap`, `settlementTreasuryCap`), construction/upgrade ticks (`advanceBuildingConstructions`, `startBuildingUpgrade`, `advanceSettlementUpgrades`), banking (`depositIntoBank`, `requestBankWithdrawal`, `matureBankWithdrawals`, `accrueBankInterest`), and garrison recruitment (`eligibleRecruitSources`, `recruitUnits`).
- **Enemy occupation blocking:** When an enemy hero (`ownerId !== settlement.ownerId`) stands on `(district.q, district.r)` (`isDistrictBlockedByHero` in `packages/engine/src/settlement/producers.ts`), that district's resource production and building gold income are zeroed until the hex is cleared, while building upkeep (`buildingUpkeepRequired`) still applies.
- **District specialisation, derived (2026-11-03):** A district has an identity of its OWN, derived from the buildings it already persists — there is no `districtType` column and nothing for a player to set. `districtSpecialisation` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/districtSpecialisation.ts`) reads the district's own `buildings` (the rows migration `035` attributes to its hex), counts how many are completed producers **on one production chain** (the chain being the resource those producers make — the food chain is `farmField` + `farmhouse` + `granary`), and a district qualifies only with at least `DISTRICT_CHAIN_MIN_PRODUCERS` (4) of them **and** at least `DISTRICT_CHAIN_MIN_SHARE` (0.6) of its buildings on that chain. Below either bar the scale is exactly 1, so a mixed district — and every district the game seeds, which holds at most three distinct buildings — pays precisely what it paid before. Above the bar the effect ramps linearly with the share from 0 at the threshold to `DISTRICT_CHAIN_MAX_EFFECT` (25%) at a district that is entirely one chain, and it is **paid on the chain and taken off every other chain the district produces**: the district pays `effect` more on its chain and `effect` less on each other resource it has producers on, computed on the chain's own per-turn total (`districtProductionLines` in `packages/engine/src/settlement/produceResources.ts`, the single place a district's buildings become output for both `settlementProductionRates` and `produceSettlementResources`). Measured, so the choice is visible and not free: four `farmField`s alone go `food 19 → 23`; four farms plus a `house` (4 of 5) go `19 → 21`; four farms plus a level-1 `goldMine` trade `+2 food` for `−6 gold` (`gold 45 → 39`), and with a level-3 mine `+2 food` for `−16 gold`. The city view states it as the **City Design panel's status line** — the one panel that is on screen for every district open — from `districtSpecialisationLabel` (`CityDesignBoxManager.setSpecialisation`, supplied by `CityView.districtFocusLabel` and refreshed on the same hook as the Build/Close label): either the share and the effect (`Food district — 4 of 5 buildings on one chain: +12.5% food, -12.5% other chains here`), or `No specialisation — 3 of 4 buildings on the food chain; 4 needed`.
- **Cleanup & persistence:** Removing all buildings from a district deletes `districts["q,r"]` and omits `SettlementState.districts` when empty. A district's own metadata (`q`, `r`, `citySpots`) persists in the `settlements.districts` JSONB column (`server/migrations/029_settlement_districts.sql`), and each of its **buildings** persists as its own `settlement_buildings` row attributed to the district's hex by the nullable `district_q` / `district_r` columns (`server/migrations/035_district_building_ledger.sql`) — `NULL` means the main city, so the two grids' local `(0, 0)` can coexist. `settlementRepo` reassembles `SettlementState.districts` from those rows plus the JSONB metadata, and falls back to the JSONB `buildings` array for a district it has no rows for at all (a game whose rows predate 035 before the backfill ran, or a district the backfill's malformed-shape guards skipped); `hydrateGameState`'s JSONB path reads the column's own copy. The JSONB column keeps that `buildings` array as the fallback, so both read paths return the same districts. Before 035 the ledger could hold only the main city — the old unique key `(game_id, settlement_id, gx, gy)` rejected a district building at its own `(0, 0)` with a duplicate-key violation (playthrough finding E8).

- **Charter placement mode:** valid hexes highlighted with green dashed outline.
- **Minimap:** resource tiles shown as an amber dot in the corner of the tile cell.
- **District roads:** each district's workforce leaves tracks on the ground joining it to the settlement's own tile — `0` workforce is no road at all, `15` of `100` is `3` tracks, a fully staffed district is the `20` cap (`WORKFORCE_PER_ROAD` in `packages/engine/src/settlement/roadNetwork.ts`; `settlementRoadNetwork(settlement, map)` in the same file computes the layout). The algorithm grows the network outward from the settlement tile in distance order — the order districts had to be founded in, since a district may only be founded touching the centre or an existing district (`packages/engine/src/settlement/districtConnectivity.ts`) — and gives each district the cheapest route to a node already in the network: the centre, or a district that joined earlier. That is the build order the map can still see: a district links to the neighbour it grew out from and shares its trunk, so the network reads as a tree rooted at the settlement. Routes are A* over the live map (`findPath`), so a road bends around mountains, dense forest and rivers without a bridge. A district with no walkable route gets no road **except a harbour**: a water district holding a `shipyard` is not walkable, so it joins the network by a single pier/bridge edge to the adjacent node it was founded against, and any district whose only way out of the water is that harbour gets the same landing edge — so a strait is crossed through its shipyard (F4, round 2 district-road card) rather than left silent, and the pier is drawn in the same two-tone hairline as every land road. Emptying a district re-runs the whole layout: a chain that hung off it re-routes to whatever is now nearest (a shorter path kills the old one), and a road that only existed to reach it stops being laid. Each edge is drawn from the tile it belongs to: a tile's road comes from its own 6-bit edge mask (the same shape the river tiles use), the connected edges are paired into through-paths, and each pair is one curve that leaves its edge perpendicularly — so a corner is one rounded elbow and a T, a Y or a crossing is what the geometry does when three sides are set, rather than a piece someone drew. Two tiles either side of a shared edge both leave that edge perpendicular, and on this projection the perpendicular is the same inward radius for both, so the road reads as one road across the boundary. There is no bow and no per-pair hash any more: a road runs along its tile's own edges and radii. The geometry, the seam invariant and the width mapping are `src/render/scene/roadGraph.ts`, and the full render description is [`src/render/docs/technical-spec.md`](../src/render/docs/technical-spec.md) §2.5.4. Purely decorative **in the scene graph** — no persistence — emitted as `settlementRoad` scene nodes before every overlay and entity layer so art always covers the road that leads to it; render details in [`src/render/docs/technical-spec.md`](../src/render/docs/technical-spec.md) §2.5.4. **It is no longer true that a road has no movement cost:** entering a hex a road crosses costs `ROAD_MOVEMENT_MULTIPLIER` (`0.75`, `packages/engine/src/map/gameMap.ts`) of that hex's terrain cost. That discount is a **modifier on the engine's one movement-cost table** (`GameMap.cost`) rather than a second cost path, so `findPath`'s A*, `computePathCost`, the reachability split and the AI's step price all read the same number, and because it multiplies it can never turn an impassable hex into a passable one — the harbour's pier over water is drawn and stays unwalked. The layering is installed wholesale from the live districts by `syncSettlementRoads(map, settlements)` (`packages/engine/src/settlement/roadNetwork.ts`) where a game's settlements and its map meet: the client's `GameStateManager` (so the adventure view's route preview and the local AI tick pay it) and the server's per-pass map in `server/app/aiDriver.ts` plus `AdvanceCharterTravel`'s recompute in `server/app/commandHandler.ts`. Terrain generation and `MapGenVersion` are untouched — roads are layered onto an already-generated map (`test/engine/roadMovement.test.ts` pins both the discount and a byte-identical-against-main generation hash).

  - **The workforce reading, measured (F2, round 2 district-road card):** *(the measurement as it stood before the 2026-11-03 road study — the mapping it describes has since been replaced, see the next bullet)* the band's width is the reading — `ROAD_WIDTH_BASE` 0.55px + `ROAD_WIDTH_PER_STRAND` 0.05px per track, capped at `ROAD_WIDTH_MAX` 1.5px (`src/render/scene/paint2d/index.ts`), so **1 track draws 0.60px and 20 tracks 1.50px on a 55.4px hex** (`HEX_SIZE` 32 × √3) — a 0.90px delta, 1.6% of the pitch, under one screen pixel at every gameplay zoom. The count is **not** a fake reading: it follows the district's actual staffing (`15%` → 3 tracks → 0.70px; `100%` → 20 tracks → 1.50px). What pins the common case flat was the staffing convention upstream: **every district of a game nobody has staffed is genuinely at 100%**, because `applyPlaceBuildings` places a new building with `workers: undefined` unless the settlement already has an explicit count (`freshPlacementWorkers(s)`, `packages/engine/src/settlement/workforce.ts` — `undefined` in a pure bootstrap town, an explicit `0` once it is being grown, and the client's own optimistic write reads the same helper through `withFreshPlacementStaffing`, `src/screens/settlements/cityView/syncedBuildings.ts`), and `undefined` is the legacy auto-staffed (max) crew — `districtWorkforcePercent` reads it as `max`, exactly as `effortFraction` does. So the only reading a player reliably got was *road vs no road*. Decided (not faked): the width mapping stays as the carrier rather than inventing variation the data does not have. **Resolved 2026-11-03 (the workforce rebalance) — the reading is now live for any district the player grows, and stays a flat band only on the free bootstrap:** `WORKFORCE_PER_POPULATION` moved 100 → 300 so the pool actually binds in a built-out city (see [Workforce staffing](#workforce-staffing-2026-10-17) above), a new placement into a settlement that has **any district** now starts at `workers: 0` (not the auto crew), and the auto crew is stated rather than silent. The consequence for this reading: a district's `districtWorkforcePercent` is no longer pinned at 100% by default — buildings the player adds to a district are explicit and start dark, and staffing them is a real (now scarce) choice, so the band's width tracks what the player did. The one case that still reads flat is a **seeded district in a game nobody has staffed** — that is the auto crew doing its bootstrap job on the town the game handed the player, not a faked reading, and it is now named in the workforce panel instead of hidden.

  - **Resolved 2026-11-03 (the road study): the width mapping moved out of the sub-pixel range.** The F2 finding above was right and it was the whole problem: at 0.60-1.50px the band is a scratch on real terrain, so a busy trunk and a light lane were not merely hard to tell apart — neither could be seen. Two independent studies built the missing half (junction geometry) and both measured the same conclusion: a perfect branch renderer still shows the player nothing at that width. The mapping is now `ROAD_WIDTH_BASE` 1.5px + 0.05px per track capped at `ROAD_WIDTH_MAX` 2.5px, with a 1.0px casing each side, so **1 track draws a 1.55px body under a 3.55px band and 20 tracks a 2.50px body under a 4.50px band** — the same 0.05px-per-track delta, now on a base the eye can see. Against the shipped river art, measured across the shared hex edge at the alpha-64 contour: the **small stream's body is 14.0px** and the **wide stream's 18.0px**, so the widest road band is still 3.1x thinner than the smallest stream — "much thinner than the streams" is met with room to spare. The measurement (and the method that produced it, which is the reason `14.0` and `18.0` are the numbers here rather than the 19/23 or 12/15 other reports give) is in `.agentboard/road-reports/synthesis/index.html`; the render constants are in `src/render/scene/roadGraph.ts`.

## Persistence

`activeCharters`/`nextCharterId`/`nextSettlementId` are all persisted server-side. `server/migrations/009_granular_entities.sql` added a `charters` table plus `games.next_charter_id`/`next_settlement_id` counter columns; `server/persistence/hydrate.ts` reads all three into `GameState` on its granular path, and the `StartCharter` command (`server/app/commandHandler.ts`) writes them via `charterRepo.upsertMany` and `gameRepo.saveHeroesAndSettlements`'s extra param. Charter *founding* is also server-authoritative: `advanceCharters()` runs as part of `EndTurn`'s round-wrap pipeline (`server/app/turnService.ts`), and that command's case syncs the result into `charterRepo`.

The CLIENT gets the same charters: `GET /api/games/:name` attaches the game's `charters` rows to its payload and `src/io/hydrateClientGame.ts` maps them onto `GameState.activeCharters`, so a reload keeps an in-flight charter instead of hydrating `activeCharters: []` (which used to strand the chartering hero: `heroes.<id>.isChartering` survived while nothing could ever advance or finish the charter). A hero left flagged `isChartering` with no charter row behind it is unlocked by hydration (`hydrateGameState`'s `unlockOrphanedCharteringHeroes`). `GameSessionManager.loadGame` runs `advanceAutoTravel()` once after a load, so a reloaded `traveling` charter resumes its route.

Charter *travel* is server-authoritative too, on the day tick: `advanceCharters(state, map)` steps every `traveling` charter one hex toward its target (through `stepTravelCharter`, other heroes' hexes blocked), so a charter whose client never sent its `AdvanceCharterTravel` steps still arrives and founds. The client's `TurnController.advanceAutoTravel()` walk still exists and simply gets there sooner inside a turn's movement budget — it POSTs its steps one at a time, in route order, because the server validates each step's `fromTile` against the hero's persisted hex (a concurrent burst was rejected 409 `hero_not_at_fromTile`).
