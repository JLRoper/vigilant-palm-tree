# Player Guide

<!-- docs:verified 2026-10-18 -->

## Start a Game

New Game supports **1–4 human players**, **0–3 AI enemies**, and five map sizes (**Small** 24×18, **Medium** 36×27, **Large** 48×36, **Huge** 72×54, **Epic** 120×90). Pick one of the playable registry factions: Human, Ashen, Ironmark, or Verdant. Neutral monsters are not a selectable player faction. AI seats are not lobby seats; games with AI enemies are driven by the server. The optional **Uncharted mode** toggle starts the map hidden: never-explored tiles render fully dark, and explored tiles stay revealed for your seat as you move.

## Move and Found Settlements

Select your hero, then click a destination to preview and request a path. The route preview shows reachable movement for the current turn; terrain changes movement costs. Double-click a settlement to enter its city view. The settlement panel's banner is the same door — double-click it too. Either way the city opens only for your own settlement on your own turn: a foreign or neutral settlement's banner shows no zoom cursor and does nothing.

To charter a settlement, use a selected hero on your turn and meet the visible requirements: a **2,500 gold** hero purse, **20 wood** and **15 stone** in the friendly provisioning settlement's warehouse, a valid passable target at least **4 hexes** from existing settlements, and no conflicting active charter. The hero travels to the target and construction takes **10 days**. The server-driven AI uses the same charter requirements and travel rules.

## Settlements and Logistics

Build from the city palette, recruit from eligible completed troop buildings, and manage the garrison from the settlement panel. Every recruit building in the settlement and its districts is also gathered into one **recruitment panel** (click **⚔ Recruit** in the City Design box, or press `r`): rows are grouped by building type — one **FARMHOUSE** block however many farmhouses you own, showing that type's ready/pool total, its **weekly refill rate** (`+2.6/wk` — what staffing buys you) and its cost icons per unit — and each type has a **Buy all** that spends as much as your pools and treasury allow, taking from the fullest building of that type first and spreading the rest evenly once they level out. A `+` buys one, shift-click buys that unit's maximum, and hovering a unit, a building line or a treasury icon explains it in a tooltip instead of on-panel text. Each building of the type keeps its own **worker stepper** (worker sprite, `n/max`, `−`/`+`) so you can staff recruiters to refill their draft pool faster and watch the weekly rate move with it — the same assignment rules as the workforce panel, including the first-click conversion to explicit crews. The panel also shows the settlement's **Garrison** — the very same section as the settlement panel's (one shared component, so a change in either place appears in both at once). Both are square tiles you drag and drop: press and drag a tile onto another slot in the same grid to **reposition** that platoon, or drag it onto the hero's **Army** grid (the hero has to be standing on the settlement) to move those troops to the hero — and drag the other way to move them back into the garrison. **Clicking a building's row** — the FARMHOUSE heading when you own one of them, a per-building line when you own several, or a unit's own row (the Peasant art) — opens that building's own menu, exactly as clicking it in the city does, right next to the building, with the building ringed until you close the menu; a unit row opens the building it recruits from (its fullest source, the same one `+` draws on), and anything that is a button (the steppers, `Buy all`, `+`) keeps its own meaning. That is the same menu with its workers, draft stock and upgrade button, so the panel stays about recruiting and the detail stays one click away. The panel's building list **scrolls** inside it, and the panel itself is **resizable**: drag the grip in its bottom-right corner and it remembers the size for next time. `Esc` closes it before the city view, and it closes with the city. Each settlement has a **worker pool** of `floor(population / 100)` you can assign per building — staff workers to scale production, market gold, recruitment draft recharge (higher-tier units refill more slowly), and town-hall upgrade speed (press `w`, or click **♟ Workforce** in the City Design box, to open the workforce panel; assigning `0` shuts a building down). Press and hold on a staffed building's grid footprint, then drag the worker sprite to another building and release to move one worker; dragging a workforce-panel chip onto a building still adds one from the pool (a plain click on a chip does nothing, and chips are disabled when no workers are idle). Dropping on empty ground, the same building, a full building, off-canvas, or onto any panel/HUD — or pressing `Esc` — cancels without changes. `Esc` closes the workforce panel before the city view, and the panel closes with the city. A drag's trailing click does not open the building menu. Hovering a panel row highlights that building in the city, and the building menu's gold/food lines show the value at the current staffing. The first assignment in a settlement converts it to explicit crews: the building you touch gets your count, and the rest of the pool is put to work automatically — jobs first (food, other production, market, recruits, town hall), then houses — so you adjust from a working city rather than an idle one; a move from a legacy building leaves its displayed count minus one at the source and `1` at the destination. Settlements you never touch keep their legacy auto-staffing. Each settlement also owes a daily wage of `1 gold per 100 population`; it simply stops when the treasury is empty (no debt, no morale penalty). The settlement panel leads with the settlement's own name and tier, then shows the treasury as `gold / cap`, the food stock with its signed daily balance (`+12.5/day` while farms out-produce the mouths, amber and negative when they do not — hover for the made-vs-eaten breakdown), and a warehouse list whose every row carries its own signed daily rate: production from tile rates and producers minus building upkeep (wood, stone) or population food. A draining stock reads amber, so the panel never shows a bare number that cannot say which way it is moving. Six resource types exist: gold, wood, stone, iron, arcane, and food. Food is not generated on world-map tiles; food spots/buildings, stockpiles, consumption, and weekly troop upkeep are live.

Hover only the bold settlement name in the Logistics panel to show stockpiles in a floating popup whose top-left corner follows the pointer, clamped only at an 8px viewport margin; keyboard focus anchors it below the name, and Escape dismisses it. Moving onto counts or buttons hides stockpiles even while the name retains focus, leaving native count tooltips unobstructed. Resource icons run vertically: treasury gold first, then wood, stone, iron, arcane, and food. Each row shows stored/capacity and signed production per turn (`+0/turn` when idle), not a net income or upkeep balance. Regular-weight supply wagon and treasure cart counts remain to the right of the settlement name, outside the popup: these are available unassigned counts from the owner's player-wide shared pools, not settlement-owned vehicles. Each count is immediately followed by its inline **Buy wagon** or **Buy cart** button; the header wraps on narrow screens. Prices appear only on button hover or keyboard focus, in a pointer-top-left popup (below the button for keyboard focus) with gold and wood icons beside **200** and **5**; button labels contain no prices.

Wagons carry physical caravan cargo between compatible routes. New games do not teleport resources through shared storage; deliveries wait for destination capacity. Hero and garrison upkeep is charged weekly, so check food stocks and income before the charge.

## Battles

- As the human attacker in a hero battle, choose **Fight**, **Quick Resolve**, or **Flee**.
- As the human defender of an AI-offered battle, choose **Fight** or **Quick Resolve**; **Flee** is unavailable because the AI move is already committed.
- Against a non-empty settlement garrison, the human attacker chooses **Assault**, **Auto-resolve**, or **Cancel**. Cancel leaves the hero on the settlement tile and does not submit a battle result.
- Defeat removes a hero; retreat relocates the hero with troops lost; surrender relocates the hero with troops retained. Settlement battles use the same hero outcome rules.

## Keyboard and Mouse

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| `?` | Open keyboard and mouse help |
| `Esc` | Clear the selected hero/settlement panel; in city view, close menus, cancel placement, or leave the city |
| `B` | Toggle the city build palette |
| `W` | Toggle the workforce panel (city view) |
| `R` | Toggle the recruitment panel (city view) |
| `Delete` / `Backspace` | Remove the building under the cursor in city view |
| Click / double-click | Select, move, attack, or enter a settlement |
| Drag / wheel | Pan / zoom the map (maximum zoom-out width is configurable from **20 to 34 hexes wide**, default **20**, under Settings → Visual) |
