# Player Guide

<!-- docs:verified 2026-10-26 -->

## Start a Game

New Game supports **1–4 human players**, **0–3 AI enemies**, and eight map sizes (**Small** 24×18, **Medium** 36×27, **Large** 48×36, **Huge** 72×54, **Epic** 120×90, **Colossal** 180×135, **Titanic** 240×180, **Gigantic** 300×225). Pick one of the playable registry factions: Human, Ashen, Ironmark, or Verdant. Neutral monsters are not a selectable player faction. AI seats are not lobby seats; games with AI enemies are driven by the server. The optional **Uncharted mode** toggle starts the map hidden: never-explored tiles render fully dark, and explored tiles stay revealed for your seat as you move. A new game now defaults to **one human seat, one AI enemy, a Medium map, and Uncharted mode on** -- every field stays editable before you create it.

**Game mode** is the second thing that changes a new game's opening position, and both creation surfaces (the home screen's Create a New Game panel and the toolbar's New Game dialog) offer it:

- **Classic** — a castle, a commander and a town per seat; expand by **chartering** new settlements (the long-standing rules).
- **Settlers** — bare wilderness: no castle and no town for anyone, not even neutral ones. Every seat starts with **one covered wagon** carrying a starting stockpile. A wagon **founds** a settlement where it stands, and the wagon **becomes that settlement's first hero** — so founding widens your territory without spending your explorer.

The form opens on the mode you last created a game with (a stored preference; picking a mode for one game does not change it), and the mode is fixed for that game's lifetime.

## Move and Found Settlements

Select your hero, then click a destination to preview and request a path. The route preview shows reachable movement for the current turn; terrain changes movement costs. A click is a toggle: click a hero or settlement to select it (its panel opens), and click that same hero or settlement again to put it down - after moving a hero, click it once more to deselect it. Clicking a different hero or settlement switches selection instead, and with only a settlement selected, a click on empty ground also puts it down. Double-clicking a settlement still enters its city view. Double-click a settlement to enter its city view. The settlement panel's banner is the same door — double-click it too, and a row in the **⌂ Settlements** roster opens it the same way (the map is centred on that settlement first). Either way the city opens only for your own settlement on your own turn: a foreign or neutral settlement's banner shows no zoom cursor and does nothing. Pointing at a hex opens the **tile panel** for it, and clicking a hex inspects it the same way: the title is the hex's coordinates, then its terrain and move cost, then whatever stands there — a resource deposit (worked or unclaimed), a settlement, a district, a hero, an in-flight charter, the territory it belongs to — and a hex you have not seen yet says **Unexplored** and nothing else. The panel and the dashed ring around the inspected hex always describe the same hex, and the panel stays on the last hex you pointed at until you point at another one (its × closes it until you do).

To charter a settlement, use a selected hero on your turn and meet the visible requirements: a **2,500 gold** hero purse, **20 wood** and **15 stone** in the friendly provisioning settlement's warehouse, a valid passable target at least **4 hexes** from existing settlements, and no conflicting active charter. The hero travels to the target and construction takes **10 days**. The server-driven AI uses the same charter requirements and travel rules.

In a **settlers** game that button reads **⛺ Found Settlement** and does something else: select your covered wagon (a wagon can carry no army, but it moves and reveals fog like a hero) and press it — that is the whole action. Founding happens **in place**, on the hex your wagon is standing on; there is no second click and no hex to pick. The town inherits the wagon's cargo, gets a name, its own rates and city spots derived from where it stands, and starts with **no buildings at all** — its city view opens on an empty plot, so the free town hall is your first placement and everything else is paid for. The wagon itself becomes that town's **first hero** (`p<seat>-hero`), still selected and still yours to move. A wagon must stand at least **3 hexes** from every settlement, on ground a settlement can occupy, and only a covered wagon can found: once a wagon has founded, its hero cannot found again. The button refuses before anything happens if the rule fails, and says why; the server re-checks the same rule, so a refusal reads with the same sentence.

## Settlements and Logistics

Build from the city palette, recruit from eligible completed troop buildings, and manage the garrison from the settlement panel. Every recruit building in the settlement and its districts is also gathered into one **recruitment panel** (click **⚔ Recruit** in the City Design box, or press `r`): rows are grouped by building type — one **FARMHOUSE** block however many farmhouses you own, showing that type's ready/pool total, its **weekly refill rate** (`+2.6/wk` — what staffing buys you) and its cost icons per unit — and each type has a **Buy all** that spends as much as your pools and treasury allow, taking from the fullest building of that type first and spreading the rest evenly once they level out. A `+` buys one from the fullest building, shift-click buys that unit's maximum across every building of the type at once, and hovering a unit, a building line or a treasury icon explains it in a tooltip instead of on-panel text. Each unit row is the **parent** for that unit: its art, ready/pool total, `+N/wk` rate and a **worker stepper** (worker sprite, `n/max`, `−`/`+`) covering every building of that type at once — one click puts one worker on each of them (or takes one off each), so you can staff the whole group without expanding it, under the same assignment rules as the workforce panel, including that an assignment only ever changes the buildings you touch. The panel also shows the settlement's **Garrison** — the very same section as the settlement panel's (one shared component, so a change in either place appears in both at once). Both are square tiles you drag and drop: press and drag a tile onto another slot in the same grid to **reposition** that platoon, or drag it onto the hero's **Army** grid (the hero has to be standing on the settlement) to move those troops to the hero — and drag the other way to move them back into the garrison. A hero always keeps at least one troop: dragging its **last** tile into the garrison is refused (the tile snaps back), because an army-less hero cannot fight or defend itself. **Clicking the unit's image row** accordions down one line per building that trains it — by default those building lines are hidden — and it never opens a building's own menu. Only a **building line** inside that dropdown is a door: clicking it opens that building's own menu, exactly as clicking it in the city does, right next to the building, with the building ringed until you close the menu; anything that is a button (the steppers, `Buy all`, `+`) keeps its own meaning. That is the same menu with its workers, draft stock and upgrade button, so the panel stays about recruiting and the detail stays one click away. The panel's building list **scrolls** inside it, and the panel itself is **resizable**: drag the grip in its bottom-right corner and it remembers the size for next time. `Esc` closes it before the city view, and it closes with the city. The **City Design** panel is a normal floating panel too — drag it by its title bar and it stays where you put it, and its own corner grip resizes it; the same grip and remembered size cover every other floating panel and menu (the hero and settlement panels and their rosters, the workforce panel, the build palette, building and selection menus, tile info, the game log, and the Logistics, Battle Results and Keyboard & Mouse windows). Each settlement has a **worker pool** of `floor(population / 100)` you can assign per building — staff workers to scale production, market gold, recruitment draft recharge (higher-tier units refill more slowly), town-hall upgrade speed, and a new building's construction speed (a fully staffed site goes up twice as fast) (press `w`, or click **♟ Workforce** in the City Design box, to open the workforce panel; assigning `0` shuts a building down). Press and hold on a staffed building's grid footprint, then drag the worker sprite to another building and release to move one worker; dragging a workforce-panel chip onto a building still adds one from the pool (a plain click on a chip does nothing, and chips are disabled when no workers are idle). Dropping on empty ground, the same building, a full building, off-canvas, or onto any panel/HUD — or pressing `Esc` — cancels without changes. `Esc` closes the workforce panel before the city view, and the panel closes with the city. A drag's trailing click does not open the building menu. Hovering a panel row highlights that building in the city, and the building menu's gold/food lines show the value at the current staffing. The first assignment in a settlement is now local: the building you touch gets your count and every other building keeps the staffing it already had (untouched settlements run their whole city at full legacy effort, shown as `Auto`), so a city converts to manual crews one building at a time and you can see what each step costs — the first touch used to convert the whole settlement and hand the rest of the pool out automatically, which gutted a level-1 city's output from one click; a move from a legacy building leaves its displayed count minus one at the source and `1` at the destination. Settlements you never touch keep their legacy auto-staffing. Each settlement also owes a daily wage of `1 gold per 100 population`; it simply stops when the treasury is empty (no debt, no morale penalty). The settlement panel is titled with the settlement's own name (its owner beside it) and labels its tier under the banner, then shows the treasury as `gold / cap`, the food stock with its signed daily balance (`+12.5/day` while farms out-produce the mouths, amber and negative when they do not — hover for the made-vs-eaten breakdown), and a warehouse list whose every row carries its own signed daily rate: production from tile rates and producers minus building upkeep (wood, stone) or population food. A draining stock reads amber, so the panel never shows a bare number that cannot say which way it is moving. Six resource types exist: gold, wood, stone, iron, arcane, and food. Food is not generated on world-map tiles; food spots/buildings, stockpiles, consumption, and weekly troop upkeep are live.

Hover only the bold settlement name in the Logistics panel to show stockpiles in a floating popup whose top-left corner follows the pointer, clamped only at an 8px viewport margin; keyboard focus anchors it below the name, and Escape dismisses it. Moving onto counts or buttons hides stockpiles even while the name retains focus, leaving native count tooltips unobstructed. Resource icons run vertically: treasury gold first, then wood, stone, iron, arcane, and food. Each row shows stored/capacity and signed production per turn (`+0/turn` when idle), not a net income or upkeep balance. Regular-weight supply wagon and treasure cart counts remain to the right of the settlement name, outside the popup: these are available unassigned counts from the owner's player-wide shared pools, not settlement-owned vehicles. Each count is immediately followed by its inline **Buy wagon** or **Buy cart** button; the header wraps on narrow screens. Prices appear only on button hover or keyboard focus, in a pointer-top-left popup (below the button for keyboard focus) with gold and wood icons beside **200** and **5**; button labels contain no prices.

Wagons carry physical caravan cargo between compatible routes. New games do not teleport resources through shared storage; deliveries wait for destination capacity. Hero and garrison upkeep is charged weekly, so check food stocks and income before the charge.

Resources are moved between your own agents by dragging, not by menus. Two heroes of yours standing next to each other can **trade** in the field: select one and **click the hex the other is standing on** (heroes can never share a hex, so trying to walk into one is how you start a trade) — or use **Trade with `<name>`…** in its hero panel — and the **Trade** window opens with both heroes side by side: their gold purse, the five cargo resources and their 4×2 army grids. Drag anything from one column onto the other to offer it (drag it back, or click its `×`, to take it off the table), use a staged row's `−`/`+` to hand over only part of a stack (a tenth of it per click, one unit for troops), and click **Confirm trade** to hand both sides over **at once**. A trade never happens in halves: if either hero cannot pay or has no room to receive, nothing moves and the window says why. A hero always keeps its army — offering away every last troop greys out **Confirm trade** and says which hero would be left with none (swapping armies with the other hero is fine). The window closes itself once the two heroes are no longer on adjacent hexes.

## Battles

- As the human attacker in a hero battle, choose **Fight**, **Quick Resolve**, or **Flee**.
- As the human defender of an AI-offered battle, choose **Fight** or **Quick Resolve**; **Flee** is unavailable because the AI move is already committed.
- Against a non-empty settlement garrison, the human attacker chooses **Assault**, **Auto-resolve**, or **Cancel**. Cancel leaves the hero on the settlement tile and does not submit a battle result.
- Defeat removes a hero; surrender relocates the hero with troops retained. Retreat opens in battle round 2 (round 3 when assaulting a settlement): only platoons standing on your starting edge column escape, the rest are lost, and the hero relocates and cannot move until the day after next. Both retreat and surrender end the hero's movement for the day. Settlement battles use the same hero outcome rules.
- If a battle result cannot be recorded (after automatic retries on network errors), a "Couldn't record this battle" card offers **Return to map**, which restores the hero's pre-battle position.

## Keyboard and Mouse

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| `?` | Open keyboard and mouse help |
| `Esc` | Close the panel you last opened or clicked (any panel or window, including Settings, Load Game and Logistics); with no panel open, clear the selected hero/settlement panel; in city view, cancel a placement, clear the tile selection, or leave the city |
| `B` | Toggle the city build palette |
| `W` | Toggle the workforce panel (city view) |
| `R` | Toggle the recruitment panel (city view) |
| `Delete` / `Backspace` | Remove the building under the cursor in city view |
| Click / double-click | Select, move, attack, or enter a settlement |
| Drag / wheel | Pan / zoom the map (maximum zoom-out width is configurable from **20 to 34 hexes wide**, default **20**, under Settings → Visual) |

Every floating panel and window is closed by `Esc`, wherever it came from: the hero and settlement panels and their rosters, tile info, the city view's design box, workforce and recruitment panels and build palette,
building and selection menus, the game log, Settings, New Game, Load Game, Logistics, Keyboard & Mouse, Battle Results and Test Battle Setup. One `Esc` closes one panel - the one on top, i.e. the one you opened or clicked last -
and only when nothing is open does it fall back to clearing the selected hero or settlement (and, in the city, to cancelling a placement and then leaving the city). A tooltip inside a window takes the first `Esc`; the
window itself takes the next one. The City Design box is the deliberate exception: the city view owns it, so `Esc` there leaves the city rather than stranding you without Build or Back.
